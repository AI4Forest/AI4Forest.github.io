**Speaker:** Assistant Professor Nico Lang

**Date:** November 12, 2026, 10:30 CET

### Abstract

Knowing the global distribution of vegetation structure is an essential prerequisite to better quantify, model, and manage carbon, water, energy, and biodiversity in the Earth system. Despite its recognized importance, vertical structure measurements remain sparse at global scales. Data-driven approaches offer great potential for integrating data from different space missions, overcoming the limitations of individual sensors, and learning generalizing representations. However, many Earth observation (EO) tasks, particularly in environmental remote sensing, remain challenging because of the limited availability of reference data and its geographic and temporal biases.

In this talk, I will first present our recent research on computing a vertical vegetation structure model of the Earth. By combining optical satellite imagery with sparse spaceborne lidar data, we go beyond canopy height to develop a three-dimensional representation of vegetation structure. I will demonstrate how this representation improves sensitivity to fine-scale structural variations, for example, enabling the identification of forest naturalness. Second, I will discuss the potential of multimodal EO data for representation learning and introduce MMEarth-Bench, a multimodal benchmark dataset for environmental remote sensing. I will present an evaluation of existing pretrained models and introduce our test-time adaptation approach using multimodal reconstruction as an adaptation signal.
